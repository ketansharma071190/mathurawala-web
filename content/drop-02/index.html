Why no onion or garlic? — the Braj culture post

Humse aksar poochha jaata hai: "Bhai, pyaaz nahi? Lahsun nahi?" Nahi. Aur yeh koi limitation nahi hai. Mathura aur Vrindavan mein sadiyoon se khana aise hi banta hai. Sattvik. Bina pyaaz, bina lahsun. Kyunki yeh Krishna ka sheher hai. Yahi Braj cuisine hai. Yahi hamare kachori, bedai aur chaat mein hota hai. Yeh taste hai. Yeh tradition hai. Radhe Radhe 🦚

Reels to film

Two made-live scripts for weeks 3-4.

Reel 1 — Chole Bhature (plate reveal)

30 seconds. Works with just one person behind the counter and a phone.

Shot 1 (3s): Close on the kadhai. Chole bubbling, steam rising. No words yet.
Shot 2 (4s): The bhature puffing up in hot oil, rotating slowly.
Shot 3 (3s): Hands lifting out the bhature, draining, setting on the plate.
Shot 4 (5s): Chole ladled over. Onion and lemon placed (garnish only, not in the recipe).
Shot 5 (3s): Wide shot of the full plate sliding across the counter.
Shot 6 (10s): First bite — fork/spoon going in, pulling chole-soaked bhature up slowly.
Text overlay at end: "Chole Bhature · Baner, Pune · ₹140"
Audio: ambient kitchen sounds, no music needed. Or a light tabla loop.

Reel 2 — No onion, no garlic (counter explainer)

20 seconds. One shot, one message. Can film at the kachori frying station.

Shot 1 (5s): Hands kneading dough or rolling kachori. Silent.
Shot 2 (5s): Kachori dropping into oil, sizzle and puff.
Shot 3 (8s): Text overlay builds word by word: "Pyaaz nahi. / Lahsun nahi. / Sirf Mathura ka swad."
Final frame (2s): Mathurawala logo on cream background.
Caption: "Braj cuisine, sadiyoon se aise hi. #mathurawala #kachori #brajfood #sattvik"
