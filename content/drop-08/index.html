Post 27 — "Baarish ki shaam, bread pakoda"

Monsoon is fully settled in by mid-July. The IT crowd in Baner is back at offices and the evening commute craving for hot, crisp fried food is real. Bread pakoda is the universal Indian monsoon snack — everyone has a memory of it. We make it live, not from frozen bread. Own this moment.

Baarish ki shaam mein ek hi cheez kaam karti hai. Garam bread pakoda. Crisp coat. Soft inside. Aloo masala. Hari chutney. Kadhai se seedha, jab aap order karo. Koi frozen nahi. Koi reheated nahi. Week 15 · Thu Jul 17 · 7 PM Photo post

Post 28 — "Thursday shaam, jalebi wali"

Jalebi is the Thursday/Friday ritual sweet for people who grew up in North India. The crisp, juicy, kesar-gold jalebi made in a kadhai is something no chain does right — they all use premix. We don't. This post leans into the sensory memory angle: the specific smell of fresh jalebi hitting sugar syrup.

Jo log jaante hain, unhe pata hai. Asli jalebi woh hoti hai jo abhi bani ho. Crisp. Juicy. Kesar ka rang. Woh smell jo sirf fresh jalebi mein hoti hai. Kadhai se seedha. Jab aap order karein. Tab banti hai. Sirf tab. Koi readymade nahi. Koi premix nahi. Sirf asli. Mathurawala, Baner, Pune. Khao toh asli khao. Post 30 — "Baner ki shaam ka mukadar, wapas" (Aloo Tikki Chaat)

Aloo Tikki Chaat is consistently the crowd-puller for the 4-8 PM evening window in Baner. It photographs beautifully. This post builds on the earlier aloo tikki post (#14 in the queue) with a fresher "repeat visit" angle: the kind of chaat that makes people come back. Reel: "Hamara sheher, humara bhog" — Janmashtami Consciousness Teaser (15-20 seconds)

This is a MOOD Reel, not an explainer. Fast cuts, no talking. The goal: seed the idea that Mathurawala = Mathura = Janmashtami, months before anyone else starts their campaign. Trending audio: something devotional-atmospheric or a bhajan snippet (copyright-safe).
