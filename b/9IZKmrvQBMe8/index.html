Around breakeven in July, strongly positive in August · 16 Aug 2026

Gas is a FIXED cost, not a recipe cost. That one reclassification explains the backlog, the Mar to Jun collapse, and why August turned so hard.

The question was: if we are profitable, why do dues keep growing? Because the costing master charged gas at about Rs 3 a plate, as if it scaled with dishes. It does not. The first four days of August ran 40 % above July's daily rate and the cylinder count did not move at all, it sat at 0.92 a day throughout. The kadhai burns the same gas whether ten kachoris come out of it or a hundred. So gas belongs with rent and salaries, at ₹76,912 a month, and the recipes were carrying ₹23,716.

Restated that way: March to June lost about ₹113,966 a month at crisis gas prices with dishes off the menu and some days shut. July was close to breakeven, which matches Ketan's own read from being able to pay people down. August at the current run-rate is +₹27,779. Because gas is fixed, every extra rupee of revenue now drops through at about 53 paise.

Gas is fixed, and that is the whole story

The single biggest reason the old P&L looked healthy. The costing master allows ₹3 a plate for deep-fry, ₹2 tawa, ₹2.5 simmer, ₹6 milk-reduce. The LPG WhatsApp group records what is actually bought and paid: nine dated invoices from 11 July to 4 August totalling 24 cylinders, every one paid the same or the next day. Net of the opening delivery that is 21 cylinders consumed across those 24 days, 0.92 a day. That is 17.5 kg of gas a day for a 15-hour halwai kitchen. An earlier read of this same group put the rate at 1.27 a day; that counted five deliveries across four intervals and overstated the line by about ₹29,260 a month.

Why a delivery rupee is worth a third of a counter rupee

We receive about 52% of the online menu price after commission, merchant-funded discount and ads. To bank the same rupee as the counter the markup would have to be 92%. Actual markups run 21–49%, median about 39%. A 39% markup does not survive a 48% deduction, because the markup is on the base and the deduction is on the marked-up price.
