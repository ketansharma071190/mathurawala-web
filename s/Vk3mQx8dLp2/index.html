3 · The pricing question, settled with evidence

You were right. The only measured India-wide study (Jefferies 2022: 80 restaurants, 8 cities, 240 orders) puts the median online markup at 10-11%; over half charge under 10%; only ~20% exceed 30%. Standard practice among established chains in 2026 is 12-22%. The 60-80% figure I used has no measured source and is retracted. The extreme markups that exist are mostly inflate-then-discount games.

You were also right about parity pressure being real: Zomato's contracts until April 2026 fined restaurants 3x the price differential per order, policed by mystery shopping, until the CCI case and NRAI pushback killed the clause. It remains contested (NRAI interim-relief filing, 14 Jul 2026).

But parity itself stays wrong for us, and this is arithmetic, not opinion. The platform takes ~46-48% of the PRICE and 0% of the COST. A ₹20-parity kachori online returns about ₹2 a unit; it needs 3.5x the orders just to match today's ₹30. Nobody in the measured data prices at parity at our margin structure; the observed norm is a moderate markup, which is what we already have.

Where our online book actually sits: median markup ~35-40% (kachori +50%, kachori sabji +66%, chole bhature +26%). That is above the 12-22% practice band, not below it. So my +15% blanket rise from 4 Aug is retracted: the evidence says hold prices roughly where they are. Two exceptions worth a look, both at the top of the band with thin returns: Chole Samosa Chaat at 0% markup (₹70=₹70) should move to ~₹85, and Kesar Rabdi 100g at +5% to ~₹95. Everything else holds.

The real online levers, in order of measured value:

1. The Zomato rating, 3.5 → 4.0+. Worth 15-25% of organic orders by measured sensitivity, which at our volumes is ₹35-60k/month of gross. The Google engine (4.2, 833, compounding) proves the product earns it; the gap is listing-specific: stale dish photos (Zomato replaced 5 with AI versions), the District 3.5 listing, review velocity on-platform. This is TDP-agenda item one.

2. Ads efficiency, not ads removal. July ads were ₹34,588 = 14% of Zomato gross across two stacked campaigns (Visits Pack + Grow Max). Ads are load-bearing (your test proved it) but the stack deserves an audit: industry pattern is escalating spend with unmeasured incrementality, and one documented case shows 4,877 clicks at ₹6 with zero conversions. Ask Sakshi for campaign-level ROI, kill the weaker of the two for two weeks, measure.

3. AOV and combos, not unit price. Online AOV is already ₹280. A ₹149 kachori-sabji + jalebi + chai combo and a ₹99 breakfast combo online lift the ticket without touching any anchor price. Combos also hide unit-price comparison, which is the standard answer to your anchoring concern.

4. DotPe direct for repeats. We keep ~97% there. The pedha-box QR and counter cards already point at us; every 5 points of mix moved to direct is worth ~₹14k/month at current volume.

5 · The playbook your vision maps to

Every brand that did what you want to do ran the same four moves:

1. Coin price, visible generosity. Balaji gave 25-30g at ₹10 when Lays gave 12g, paid kiranas 20% margin, spent nothing on TV, and reached ₹5,454 crore at >10% net margin. Our version: the ₹20 kachori stays ₹20 and LOOKS generous: sabji served visibly, the pouch branded.

2. Industrialize ONE hero SKU before outlet two. Chitale machined bakarwadi in 1996 (200 kg/day with 40 people → 2,000 kg/hour on one machine) and only then scaled 8 → ~125 stores, zero external capital. The kachori is our bakarwadi. Batch yield, dough sheeter, standard fill weight, so outlet two inherits a machine, not a cook's hands.

3. Hub and spoke. Bikanervala fed every Delhi outlet from one Lawrence Road kitchen from the 1950s. Our Baner kitchen becomes the hub; future points of sale are small-format counters (Jumboking: ₹6 net on a ₹20 vada pav from 200-300 sq ft transit boxes), not full restaurants.

4. Aggregators as a channel, never the foundation. None of the legacy giants was built on delivery apps, and the modern take is 25-35%+ all-in. Use them for reach, price them properly, and pull repeats to direct.

6 · Menu cleanup: the safe sequence

The root cause is fixable in the software. PetPooja's own API shows one item can serve dine-in, pickup AND delivery (item_ordertype "1,2,3") with a separate markup_price for the online rate. The [D]/[online] duplicate items were never structurally necessary. Every item also carries a stable internal itemid separate from its display name, and an active flag, so deactivation is real and non-destructive.

The sequence (deactivate, never delete):

1. Export full-history item-wise and category-wise reports FIRST, plus the current menu. Done in effect: the margin book already carries the canonical map of 112 items.

2. Build one-canonical-item-per-product mapping (Kachori=Dal Kachori etc. already mapped).

3. RENAME the best-listed duplicate into the canonical name (keeps itemid, keeps history).

4. DEACTIVATE the rest. Never delete: PetPooja has no public documentation of what delete does to item-wise history (their help centre 404s), so get their written answer first: +91 79692 23344 / Support@Petpooja.com. Industry benchmark (Square) says transaction history survives deletes, but we do not assume it for PetPooja.

5. Cut over on a month boundary (1 Sep) so month-on-month reports stay comparable. PetPooja pushes the FULL menu to Zomato/Swiggy on every change; verify both live listings within 24-48h, especially that dish photos and item ratings survived the rename.

6. Lock it: Aman edits prices only, never creates items. New items go through you.
