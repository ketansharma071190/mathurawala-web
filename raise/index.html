The question Ketan asked

Why not aim for a $3-4M seed, like SaaS does?

Straight answer: we can aim much bigger than the ₹2 Cr we first scoped, and we should. But $3-4M is not a default we can ask for on day one. It is a number we engineer. Here is the honest logic.

Why SaaS gets $3-4M seeds

Huge TAM, 80%+ gross margins, recurring revenue, capital-light scaling, and venture-scale exits. The model itself promises a 100x outcome, so investors pay up early.

Why food usually gets less

Thinner margins, capex per outlet, linear and local scaling. A single-outlet restaurant doing ₹1.1 Cr cannot defend a $3-4M seed. That is the trap we must refuse to be put in.

The unlock: we are not pitching a restaurant. We are pitching a brand, an IP, and a category, the Haldiram model (a 1937 namkeen identity, now ~$8-10B). Consumer brands that get backed like venture (boAt, Mamaearth, The Whole Truth, Liquid Death) raised on narrative, demonstrated demand, and a scale engine with SaaS-like economics, not on outlet EBITDA. Our scale engine, packaged peda/namkeen, gifting, and diaspora D2C, has high repeat and far better margins than dine-in. That is the SaaS-shaped wedge inside food.

₹2-2.5 CrFloor · raise today on current proof
  • Defensible on ₹1.1 Cr ARR + brand
  • Believers, operator-angels, Pune/family offices
  • Funds the moat + a lean flagship + the pilot
$1.5-2MTarget · the ambitious seed (₹12-16 Cr)

  • Requires a real owned audience as proof of demand

$3-4MSwing · only when earned (₹25-33 Cr)
  • Needs demonstrated D2C/packaged traction, or
  • A strategic / celebrity anchor that re-rates the round
  • Real investor competition (multiple term sheets)

My recommendation: publicly aim for the $1.5-2M target, architect everything below to manufacture the conditions, and let competition pull us to the $3-4M swing. We never quote a valuation first; we create demand and let the market set it.

The part Ketan asked for

Outreach no one has done before.

Most founders email a deck and beg for a meeting. We will not. We turn the raise into an experience, a story, and an event that investors want into.

01

"Mathura in a Box" tasting pitch

We do not send a PDF first. We send a beautiful Mathura tasting box, fresh peda, a kachori kit, a kulhad, a mor-pankh, a handwritten note from a Mathura-born founder, and a single QR to a 90-second film. The box IS the pitch. No SaaS founder can make an investor feel something with a spreadsheet. We can make them taste home.

02

Raise the round in public

A founder-led content series, "Building Bharat's first global heritage food brand," on our channels + LinkedIn. Document the journey honestly. Demand comes inbound. The Whole Truth and Liquid Death proved attention pulls capital; we do it around a story that already makes people cry.

03

The category manifesto

Publish one sharp, quotable essay: "India's living food has no global brand, and that is the biggest unclaimed opportunity in consumer." It reframes us from a Pune outlet to the category's leader, and aligned investors raise their hand before we even reach out.

04

The 90-second founder film

Our pitch artifact is a film, not a deck. From Mathura to the world, the founder, the family, the flame, the feeling. Emotional, multisensory, forwardable. The deck is the appendix.

05

The Braj-thali investor dinner

Host a curated founder dinner, a real Braj thali, at the outlet or a Mumbai/Bengaluru pop-up, for a small set of investors and creators. Turn the raise into an event people ask to be invited to. Reverse the power dynamic.

06

Diaspora-led capital

Go to NRI / Bhartiya HNIs who feel the homesick thesis in their gut. They become investor, evangelist, and market access in one. (We will structure this carefully and within securities rules.)

07

A strategic or culturally-aligned anchor

Land one anchor, a heritage-food strategic, or a Krishna/UP-pride aligned founder or public figure, who re-rates the round and brings distribution. One believable anchor is what turns a $1.5M round into a $3-4M one.

08

Engineer the FOMO

Run a tight, time-boxed process (a ~3-week window). Cluster all the boxes, the film, the dinner and the warm intros so interest arrives at once. Scarcity + momentum = competition = the bigger number and better terms. We never look like we are waiting.

The sequence

Next 60-90 days: earn the bigger number.

Weeks 1-2 · Arsenal

Finalize the deck (done), the 90-second film script + shoot, the tasting-box design, and the manifesto draft. Build the data room (PetPooja + ARR bridge + P&L). Start the build-in-public series.

Weeks 2-6 · Demand

Grow the owned audience hard (festival content engine: Rakhi → Ganesh → Janmashtami). Open a packaged-peda / gifting waitlist or pre-order, the single most powerful proof for the scale-engine thesis. Every pre-order is a number an investor cannot dismiss.

Weeks 4-8 · The process

Ship the tasting boxes + film to a tight target set, host the dinner, publish the manifesto. Run the time-boxed window. Let competition set the price.

Weeks 8-12 · Close

Convert the warmest interest, secure an anchor, and close the target round, with the swing on the table if demand + a strategic show up.

The single biggest lever for the bigger number is demonstrated demand before the raise closes: audience + a packaged waitlist. That is what lets a food brand command a SaaS-shaped round. Build the demand, and the valuation follows.
